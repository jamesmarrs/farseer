# Farseer MCU BOM

Bill of materials for the MCU circuit drawn in `mcu-schematic.pdf` (generated by
`gen_mcu_schematic.py`). Design rationale, layout rules, and datasheet citations
live in [mcu-support-circuit.md](mcu-support-circuit.md) — this file is the
**single source of truth for parts and values**; that one explains *why*.

Scope: a **bare PIC18F57Q84 in the 48-pin TQFP**, powered from the `3V3_SYS` rail
already designed in [power-supply-bom.md](power-supply-bom.md). The Curiosity
Nano's debugger, LDOs, level shifters, and USB path are deliberately **not**
reproduced (mcu-support-circuit.md §8).

Authoritative source: `pic_resources/PIC18F27-47-57Q84-Microcontroller-Data-Sheet-DS40002213.pdf`
(**DS40002213F**), read in the **48-pin column** throughout.

**Status column** matches `power-supply-bom.md`:

- **Req** — mandatory; the part will not function (or is unsafe) without it.
- **Rec** — strongly recommended for robustness/EMC; ships without it at your peril.
- **Opt** — genuine design choice.

> `3V3_SYS` decoupling for the PIC also appears in `power-supply-bom.md` §5 as
> `C_pic` / `C_picBulk`. **The same physical parts** — listed there as rail
> distribution, listed here as MCU support. Do not order twice.

## 4. ICSP programming + debug header — DS40002213F §4.4, Figure 48-2

The mating tool is a **PICkit 5**, used for both programming and in-circuit debug
(three hardware breakpoints over the same two pins, DS40002213F p. 6). The
connector is permanent, not a manufacturing-only fixture.

| Ref | Qty | Status | Value / description | Suggested MPN (datasheet) | Notes |
|-----|-----|--------|---------------------|---------------------------|-------|
| J_icsp | 1 | Req | 6-pin 0.1 in header, **0.025 in square posts**, 0.1 in pitch | generic (Würth/Molex 0.1 in SIL) | PICkit pinout: 1 VPP/MCLR, 2 VDD, 3 VSS, 4 ICSPDAT, 5 ICSPCLK, 6 NC. Figure 48-2 note 1 requires the 0.025 in square post. **Pin 1 must be marked** — square pad + silkscreen triangle + `VPP` legend; the header is unkeyed and Figure 48-2 draws a "Pin 1 Indicator" as part of the definition. The PICkit 5's 8-pin body mates with this; its pins 7–8 are unused |
| R_icsp1, R_icsp2 | 0–2 | Rec | **47 Ω** 0603, series on PGD / PGC | generic | §4.4: "tens of ohms", **must not exceed 100 Ω**. Fit only if the connector is exposed to ESD |
| R_icspPD1, R_icspPD2 | 2 | Rec | 47 kΩ 0603, pull-down on PGD / PGC | generic | Matches the Curiosity Nano debugger side; expected by Microchip tooling |

§4.4 forbids **pull-ups, series diodes, and capacitors** on ICSPCLK/ICSPDAT —
they interfere with the programmer. RB6 (18) and RB7 (19) must carry **nothing
else on this board**.

**Header pin 2 is VDD sense, not a supply.** Power the board from `VSYS` first
and leave MPLAB's *"Power target circuit from tool"* **off**: the PICkit's target
supply is sized for a bare MCU, and this board carries the NEO-M9N, two bucks,
and a BG95 card that peaks at 2.7 A. The tool reports a target-voltage error
rather than an overload, so the cause is not obvious. See
[mcu-support-circuit.md](mcu-support-circuit.md) §5 for the full bring-up notes,
including why `LVP` (CONFIG4[5]) must be left at its erased `1`.

## 5. Debug console — USB-C + USB-to-UART bridge

Reproduces the Curiosity Nano's **virtual serial port (CDC)** so a plain USB-C
cable gives you the 115200 `printf` console from [uart1.c](../uart1.c). It does
**not** reproduce programming — the Nano does both over one cable only because it
carries an ATSAMD21 running Microchip's debugger firmware. Programming **and
in-circuit debug** stay on `J_icsp` (§4) with a PICkit 5. See
mcu-support-circuit.md §9 for the reasoning.

**Data only — USB never powers the board.** `U5` runs from `3V3_SYS`, so when the
vehicle rail is off the bridge is off and physically cannot drive the PIC's pins.

**The bridge is a Silicon Labs CP2102N-A02-GQFN24**, and its datasheet now lives
at `datasheets/Silabs_CP2102N_USB-UART_Bridge_DataSheet_Rev1.5.pdf` (**Rev 1.5**),
so every value below is cited like the rest of this BOM. The QFN24 is the variant
with **separate VIO and VDD pins** (Table 1.1, p. 2), which is what lets the whole
part sit on `3V3_SYS`.

**Why 22.1 k / 47.5 k rather than a convenient round pair.** The divider has to
land between two hard limits, both quoted in the §2.3 note (p. 9):
the VBUS pin's absolute maximum is **VIO + 2.5 V** (Table 3.10), and the voltage
that counts as "host attached" is **VIH = VIO − 0.6 V** (Table 3.7). At
VIO = 3.3 V that is a window of **2.7 V to 5.8 V**. The datasheet divider gives
5 V × 47.5 / (22.1 + 47.5) = **3.41 V** — comfortably above the 2.7 V threshold
and well under the maximum. The note also explains the second job the resistors
do: *"the current limitation of the resistor divider prevents high VBUS pin
leakage current, even though the VIO + 2.5 V specification is not strictly met
while the device is not powered"* — which is precisely our case, a live USB cable
against a board whose rails are down.

**Self-powered is Figure 2.3 plus Figure 2.6, and nothing else.** Figure 2.3
(p. 6) is the connection diagram *"when the internal 5 V-to-3.3 V regulator is not
used"*: `VREGIN` and `VDD` both go to a 3.0–3.6 V input, which Table 3.6 note 1
makes explicit — *"If the 5 V voltage regulator is not used, VREGIN should be
tied to VDD."* Figure 2.6 (p. 9) is the matching USB side, where VBUS reaches
**only the sense pin** through the divider and never `VREGIN`. Compare Figure 2.5,
the bus-powered variant, which ties VBUS to `VREGIN` — that is the wiring we must
**not** use, because it is what would let a laptop power the bridge with the
vehicle rail dead.

QFN24 pin numbers, from Table 5.2 (pp. 29–30), since the schematic only shows names:

| Function | Pin | Wired to |
|----------|-----|----------|
| `D+` / `D−` | 3 / 4 | `J_dbg` A6/B6 and A7/B7 |
| `VIO` | 5 | `3V3_SYS` |
| `VDD` | 6 | `3V3_SYS` |
| `VREGIN` | 7 | `3V3_SYS` (regulator unused) |
| `VBUS` | 8 | divider tap, `VBUS_DET` |
| `RSTb` | 9 | 1 kΩ to `VIO` |
| `RXD` | 20 | `DBG_TX` ← PIC RF0 |
| `TXD` | 21 | `DBG_RX` → PIC RF1 |
| `GND` | 2 + centre pad | ground plane |

Wiring, and the easy mistakes:

- `U5` **TXD (21)** → PIC **RF1 (pin 37, `DBG_RX`)**; `U5` **RXD (20)** ← PIC
  **RF0 (pin 36, `DBG_TX`)**. The cross is already done for you, exactly as the
  Nano wires CDC TX to the target's UART RX (DS50003011 Figure 3-1). **Do not
  cross twice.**
- D+/D− must go to **both** A6/A7 **and** B6/B7 on the receptacle, or the cable
  only works one way up.
- Route the pair as **90 Ω differential**, short, with ground either side.
- `DTR`/`RTS` are unused — no auto-reset circuit. Reset is `MCLR` via the ICSP
  header. `CTS` (18), `DSR` (22), `DCD` (24), and `RI` (1) are **inputs** and the
  firmware never uses flow control, so tie `CTS` to GND (it is active low, so
  this leaves it permanently asserted) and pull the rest to GND through 10 kΩ
  rather than leaving CMOS inputs floating. The datasheet gives no explicit
  guidance for unused control inputs — this is general practice, not a citation.
- `SUSPEND` (17) and `SUSPENDb` (15) are left unconnected. §4.1 (p. 21) notes
  both *"temporarily float high during a CP2102N reset"*, which matters only if
  something downstream is gated by them; nothing here is.
- Levels need no shifting. Both parts run from `3V3_SYS`, and the bridge's
  VOH spec of VIO − 0.7 V (Table 3.7) is quoted at a 7 mA load — against the
  PIC's ±125 nA input leakage the output sits essentially at 3.3 V, comfortably
  over the PIC's Schmitt-trigger VIH of 0.8 × VDD = 2.64 V (DS40002213F D322).

## 7. Clock — not fitted

The design runs from **HFINTOSC at 64 MHz**; [uart1.c](../uart1.c) already
derives its baud from 64 MHz Fosc. These rows exist so the footprints can be
placed as no-fit, and so nobody re-litigates the decision.

| Ref | Qty | Status | Value / description | Suggested MPN (datasheet) | Notes |
|-----|-----|--------|---------------------|---------------------------|-------|
| Y_hs | 0 | Opt | 10 MHz crystal, OSC1 (32) / OSC2 (33) | — | Only with HS/XT in CONFIG1. Costs RA6 + RA7 and buys nothing here |
| C_osc1, C_osc2 | 0 | Opt | 18 pF / 22 pF, 0402 | — | Curiosity Nano values for its 12 pF-CL crystal |
| R_s | 0 | Opt | Series drive resistor | — | §12: may be needed for low-drive-level quartz |
| Y_sosc | 0 | Opt | 32.768 kHz, SOSCI (35) / SOSCO (34) | — | Secondary oscillator unused; not populated on the Nano either |

> **Bring-up trap.** CONFIG1 erases to all 1s → `RSTOSC = 111` (EXTOSC) and
> `FEXTOSC = 111` (external clock > 8 MHz). A fresh part expects an external
> clock this board does not have. **CONFIG1 must be programmed with
> `RSTOSC = 000`** or the board will not run standalone.

## Verify before ordering

- [ ] CONFIG1 programmed with `RSTOSC = 000` so the part runs on HFINTOSC
- [ ] 0.1 µF at **both** pin pairs 7/6 and 30/31, each within 6 mm, MCU side
- [ ] Decoupling caps are X7R with SRF ≥ 200 MHz — not Y5V
- [ ] MCLR network within 6 mm of pin 20, using 10 kΩ (not the Nano's 47 kΩ)
- [ ] `C_mclr` liftable by `JP_mclr` or no-fit if the programmer objects
- [ ] Nothing but the programmer on RB6 / RB7 — no pull-ups, diodes, or caps
- [ ] `R_icsp1/2` ≤ 100 Ω if fitted
- [ ] `J_icsp` pin 1 marked (square pad + triangle + `VPP`) — the header is unkeyed
- [ ] PICkit 5 target power left **off**; board powered from `VSYS` when programming
- [ ] `LVP` left at `1` — clearing it forces high-voltage VPP entry from then on
- [ ] `U5` powered from **`3V3_SYS`, not VBUS** — USB must not power the board.
      Concretely: `VREGIN` (7) tied to `VDD` (6), Figure 2.3, **not** to VBUS
- [ ] VBUS reaches **only** the sense pin (8) through the divider — Figure 2.6,
      not the bus-powered Figure 2.5
- [ ] `R_vbus1` = 22.1 kΩ, `R_vbus2` = 47.5 kΩ, giving 3.41 V at the sense pin
- [ ] 4.7 µF **and** 0.1 µF at **each** of `VDD`, `VIO`, `VREGIN` — six caps
- [ ] 1 kΩ `RSTb` pull-up fitted, to `VIO`
- [ ] **Two separate 5.1 kΩ** CC resistors, one per CC pin
- [ ] D+/D− wired to **both** A6/A7 and B6/B7, routed as a 90 Ω pair
- [ ] Bridge TXD → RF1 and bridge RXD → RF0 — crossed exactly once
- [ ] Every unused pin driven low in firmware or pulled to VSS via 1–10 kΩ
- [ ] All PPS assignments re-checked against the **48-pin** columns of
      Table 21-1 / 21-2, not the 28/40-pin columns
- [ ] Rebuild the drawing with `./docs/build_schematics.sh mcu` after any change here
